Another year has gone by. More games have been played. Not as many as I’d hoped at the beginning of the year, but possibly enough. I believe this is the first year where I actually acheived the goal I set out: Finish The Ringmaker cycle in Lord of the Rings LCG, which I did in October. This year, due to family-related matters, I am not setting any goals. I also do not believe I will be playing much solitaire, at least through Q1.

As noted elsewhere, work priorities have consistently trumped game (or any other) priorities in the past, and there is no reason to believe this will change in 2025. My current gig is tracking to switch from contract to full time W2, which means it will take over my life. This is how it goes in the startup niche.

Updates

Roughly quarterly I jot down a few notes recapping recently game play, or lack thereof, as the case may be.

July 26, 2025

The year is turning out a bit better than I thought it would. At least up until the start of Q3 when I was on an on-call rotation from July 11 to July 25. This halted even the conception of game play during that period, although I did get some light games played before the rotation got emotionally oppressive. Hopefully I’ll get another game from my 2025 Aspirations list knocked out.

Anyways, Q2 saw further progress on 2025 Aspirations with the second to last scenario in Silver Bayonet. Got some family game time, and Star Realms is back in rotation in a big way with two different regular opponents. Got in a few games of Jump Drive as well. All in all, not bad.

April 6, 2025

Q1 was pretty good for game play. I was enticed into making a list of games for 2025, for which I listed 6 rather the 12 which was solicited. I’ve had experience with these sorts of lists in the past, evidence in many of these Mastery articles from previous years will attest to that. Since many of the games will require multiple plays to acheive the listed goal, I’m comfortable with 6 instead of 12. And indeed, I’m already 6 games into High Frontier alone, and will need at least one more play to achieve the goal of a fully competitive game with Modules 0, 1, and 2.

Likewise, Silver Bayonet is liable to require a couple of prelimnary games to get my opponent back into the rules. So while it’s 6 titles listed, it will be far more than 6 plays to get everything done.

What to play next?

Deciding what to play next is always a tough call when faced with a closet full of games, all of which were purchased with intent to play. Here's one I'm dealing with the decision.

  • Something New: this means both something new to me and a game which has been published recently, within the last two or three years.
  • Something Classic: I've acquired a number of games properly considered "classic" which haven't yet been played. The current example would be PanzerGruppe Guderian.
  • Something Again: mastery requires constant practice, which means playing games many, many times to allow nuance to appear.
  • Something Vietnam: as I'm on a Vietnam binge. I don't have all the Vietnam games ever published, but I can say that I have a lot of them, and probably more than most people.
  • Something Obscure: as it's fun to poke around in the dustier corners of game design.
  • Something Different: break away from the same old publishers and try a game from one of the new kids on the block. Or, conversely, dip into legacy with a classic tried and true.
  • Something Light: once in a while it's nice to actually finish a game all the way through in one sitting, in an hour or two.
  • Something Heavy: if possible, it's nice to set up something big and complicated which can be left out for days or weeks or even months, for a leisurely stroll through a very large or long game.

There are surely many more ways to choose, but the above method keeps me busy enough, helps me get both breadth and depth into my play, and provides me with a sense of progress.

The odd man out

When there is an odd man out:

  • Have a solo game ready to play.
  • Keep score or advise on a game in progress.
